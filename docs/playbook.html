The Volume Track

The job radar watches product companies with public job boards. It cannot see the mass-hiring drives that service companies run, and those are the ones most likely to produce a first offer quickly. This is the part that has to be set up by hand — once, in about forty minutes.

Dates are deliberately absent. Drive windows and batch eligibility change every few weeks, and the aggregator sites that republish them contradict each other constantly. Register on the official portals below and let their alerts tell you when a window opens. Treat any third-party "drive announced" post as a prompt to go check the official portal, never as the source.

1Register on the company portals

Service companies do not post fresher roles on LinkedIn or Naukri in any reliable way. They open a registration window on their own portal, run an aptitude test, and close it. If the profile is not already created, the window is usually gone before the form is done.

  • TCS — create a NextStep profile and complete it fully; off-campus applicants also go through iBegin. The NQT (National Qualifier Test) is the single test that feeds most TCS fresher hiring, so this is the highest-value registration of the set.
  • Infosys — register on the Infosys careers portal and watch for the InfyTQ and off-campus listings.
  • Wipro — the Elite NTH and Turbo hiring tracks are the fresher routes; both open through the Wipro careers site.
  • Accenture — create a profile and set a job alert for Bangalore, Associate Software Engineer.
  • Cognizant, Capgemini, LTIMindtree, Mphasis, Hexaware, Persistent — same pattern, one profile each.
  • Deloitte USI, EY, PwC, KPMG — large Bangalore technology practices that hire freshers into analyst roles and are usually overlooked by CS graduates.

Never pay for a job. No legitimate employer in India charges a fresher a registration, training or security deposit. Any "pay ₹X to confirm your offer" message is a scam, including ones that arrive on letterhead with a real company's logo.

The three Naukri searches worth saving

Save each as its own alert rather than one broad search — the results differ more than they look.

("software engineer" OR "software developer" OR SDE) AND (fresher OR "0-1 years" OR trainee) AND Bangalore
(python OR django OR fastapi OR flask) AND (fresher OR "0-1 years") AND (Bangalore OR Bengaluru)
("machine learning" OR "generative ai" OR "ai engineer" OR "data scientist") AND (fresher OR trainee OR "0-1 years") AND Bangalore

Set each to daily, not instant — instant alerts train you to ignore them. Send them all to one Gmail label so the morning check is a single place.

3Prepare the answers once

Every one of these portals asks the same twelve questions. Writing the answers once and keeping them in a notes file turns a 20-minute application into a 3-minute one, which is the difference between applying to four drives and applying to one.

  • Both resume PDFs, named YourName_Fullstack.pdf and YourName_AI_ML.pdf
  • A 60-word and a 150-word "about me" paragraph
  • 10th, 12th and current CGPA, plus percentage equivalents — most forms demand percentage
  • Scanned 10th and 12th marksheets, Aadhaar, PAN and a passport photo, in one folder
  • A one-line summary of each project, ready to paste
  • Preferred location, notice period (immediate) and expected CTC — say "as per company standards" as a fresher

4The weekly rhythm

Once set up, this is the whole routine. It should take about two hours a day.

Every morning

Open the radar dashboard, apply to the new matches. Then clear the Gmail alert label.

Every morning

Ten referral messages. Alumni from your own college first — they reply far more often than strangers.

Every evening

Ninety minutes of DSA and core CS. The drives are filtered by an aptitude test, not a resume.

Every Monday

Check the six company portals directly for a newly opened window.

Every Friday

Follow up on anything applied to more than five days ago and still silent.

Every Sunday

One GitHub commit or one LinkedIn post about a project. Recruiters check both.

5What actually converts

Worth being blunt about the arithmetic, because it changes where the two hours go. A cold portal application from a fresher converts at roughly one interview per hundred. A referral from someone inside the company converts at something closer to one in ten. The drives are a third thing again: they are a test, so preparation beats application volume entirely.

That means the ten referral messages a day matter more than the next fifty applications, and the ninety minutes of aptitude practice matter more than both. The radar exists so that finding jobs stops eating the time those two need.

One more thing worth doing this week. If your resume's project entries carry no live links, add them. For a fresher with no full-time experience, a working demo link is the single strongest signal available — it is the thing a reviewer can check in ten seconds. Filling those in is worth more than any alert on this page.
